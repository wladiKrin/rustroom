# M10.11a — Film negative inversion

**Date:** 2026-10-11 · **Branch:** `m10.11a-film-negative` · **Status:** design approved

Phase 10's M10.11a: *"Film negative inversion — invert, white balance from the film base, a tone curve
(2026-08-26)."* `docs/BACKLOG.md` records it as **a stage plus an eyedropper**, not a plugin system; the
eyedropper machinery now exists (white balance, defringe).

## Decisions (the author's)

- **Pick the film base, then an automatic balance**: one eyedropper click on the unexposed film (the
  rebate, or between frames); Rustroom then measures the frame once and sets black and white per
  channel, cancelling the orange mask and the casts. **Stored in the edits**, so Sync carries it across a
  roll; an **Auto** button measures again.
- **A stage, not a preset** (asked and answered): a preset can only flip the tone curve, which runs after
  white balance, Exposure and the tone sliders — every one of them would then work backwards — the curves
  clip their input at 1 and the composite is forced to rise, and the orange mask differs per roll.

## Design

### The inversion (`pipeline/src/stages/negative.rs`, a prefix step)

Per pixel and channel, in the linear working space the decode arrives in:

- density above the base `d = log10(base / max(pixel, ε))`;
- the scene's light `e(d) = 10^(d / γ)` — film density is `γ · log10(exposure)`, so this recovers
  exposure, scene-linear, which is what every later stage expects;
- levels `out = max(0, (e(d) − e(low)) / (e(high) − e(low))) · TARGET`, `low`/`high` the measured shadow
  and highlight **densities** of that channel, `TARGET` = 0.9 so the measured highlight sits under white
  with headroom for what is brighter.

**Black & white**: the three ratios `pixel / base` are averaged before the logarithm, and the mean of the
three `low`/`high` used, giving one channel copied to all three.

**Placement: right after lens correction, before noise reduction, dehaze, spots and geometry.** Lens
vignetting multiplies the negative and must be undone first; everything after then works on a positive —
dehaze's frame-global airlight replay includes the step, so it measures the positive too. A prefix step
is computed once and cached, so no tail slider re-runs it; it has no GPU twin (it runs on the CPU, as
spot removal does), which a tail stage would need to keep the GPU tail whole.

### The settings (`edit_model::Negative`)

`Negative { on: bool, base: [f32; 3], gamma: f32, low: [f32; 3], high: [f32; 3], mono: bool }` — default
off, γ 0.6 (a colour negative's typical contrast). Active when on, the base positive in every channel and
`high > low` in every channel.

- ⚠ **Densities, not levels**, are stored for black and white, so changing the film contrast keeps the
  measurement valid.
- **Its own edit group, "Negative" — the 11th, appended, so every existing index stays — OFF by default,
  like Geometry**: pasting a digital photo's settings or applying a colour preset must never switch a
  negative's inversion off. Sync with *Negative* ticked carries it across a roll.

### Measuring (`negative_tool`, lib)

- **The pick**: the mean of a 5 × 5 patch of the **decode buffer** under the click (the white-balance
  eyedropper's rule — the rendered texture is already inverted) becomes `base`; `on` is set.
- **The balance** (on the pick, and on *Auto*): a 512 px analysis of the decode **inside the crop**
  (`auto_tone_tool::source_crop`, so the rebate and the scanner's border can be cropped out first), the
  0.5th and 99.5th percentiles of each channel's density as `low` and `high` — so a few dust specks, which
  print white, do not set the white.
- **`positive(rgb, &Negative)`** — the stage's own per-pixel function, exported, for every reader below.

### Everything that reads the decode sees the positive

With an active negative, these read the decode buffer and would otherwise measure the negative; each goes
through `positive`:

- the **white-balance** and **defringe** eyedroppers (their patch);
- **Auto tone** (its analysis image);
- the **AI masks**' model input — and the AI cache key carries the negative, so a map computed on the
  negative is not reused on the positive;
- **red-eye detection** (its sample function).

The colour readout reads the rendered texture and needs nothing.

### Thumbnails

A Library thumbnail renders the edits over the camera's embedded JPEG, which has been through the camera's
own tone curve — a base measured on the raw data does not match it. **A RAW with an active negative is
thumbnailed from its half-size raw decode** instead (about 1 s rather than 0.1 s, only for those photos),
cached by file like the extraction. A JPEG or TIFF scan already thumbnails from its own pixels.

### The panel

A **Negative** block above the Profile block — collapsed by default, its header saying *on* or *off*, and,
like Profile, **not reset** by Reset adjustments (it is the conversion, not an adjustment): a *Film
negative* tick (enabled once a base is picked), the base swatch with *Pick film base* (arms the
eyedropper; the next click on the photo picks and balances), *Auto*, *Black & white*, and *Film contrast*
(±100, mapped to γ from 1.2 to 0.3, 0 = 0.6). Each change is one undo step.

### Placement

`edit-model` (`Negative`, the group, `merge_from`); `pipeline` (`stages/negative.rs`, `PrefixStepKey::
Negative`, the step in `build_prefix_steps`); app lib `negative_tool.rs`; the readers in `auto_tone_tool`,
`ai_worker`/`ai_cache`, `eye_detect`'s caller and the eyedroppers; `thumb_loader`; bin
`develop/panel/negative.rs`, the eyedropper in `develop/canvas/eyedropper.rs`, `Session::picking_negative`.

## Tests

- **Stage**: a synthetic scene put through a film model (density = γ·log10(exposure), transmittance × an
  orange base) inverts back to the scene within tolerance; the base itself becomes black and the densest
  measured point the target, per channel; changing γ keeps black and white where they were; Black & white
  gives equal channels; off or unpicked is the identity; a golden of a synthetic negative chart.
- **Measuring**: the pick sets the base from the decode, not the render; the balance ignores what lies
  outside the crop; dust specks above the 99.5th percentile do not move the white.
- **Readers**: the white-balance eyedropper on an inverted photo solves from the positive; Auto tone, AI
  input and red-eye sampling see the positive; the AI key differs with the negative on.
- **Groups**: Negative is off in the default groups; Sync with it ticked carries it; Reset adjustments
  leaves it.
- **Panel**: *Pick film base* arms the picker; a click picks, balances and disarms, one undo step.
- **Thumbnails**: a RAW with an active negative is thumbnailed from its decode.
- **Sabotages, each red**: the inversion without the exponent; levels from the wrong channel; the step
  after dehaze; the pick reading the render; a reader skipping `positive`; the group on by default; the
  thumbnail from the embedded JPEG.

## As built (2026-10-11)

- **A default `Negative` is not serialized** (`skip_serializing_if`): the pinned preview fingerprint showed
  a default block would change the key of every rendered preview on disk and grow every sidecar.
- **The re-measure button is *Auto balance*** — Auto tone already owns *Auto* in the same panel.
- **The AI masks**: besides the cache key, the model-input cache is keyed on the negative, the session's
  in-memory maps are dropped when the negative changes on the same photo, and a returning result is kept
  only if its key is still current.
- **RAW negative thumbnails are not disk-cached**: rendered from the float half-size decode, because the
  store's 8-bit previews would band the dense areas the inversion turns into highlights.
- **The patch and red-eye sampler** goes through `view`, so retouch previews show the positive too.
- **Known**: a RAW negative's ghost (its embedded JPEG through the negative) shows for a moment in
  approximate colour before the decode is adopted.
- **Found in use — the measurement leaves out what is not film**: densities below −0.05 (brighter than the
  base: sprocket holes, open backlight) or above 3.0 (the holder around the film), and a border of 15 % of
  each edge on an uncropped frame (2 % once cropped). An HP5 camera scan whose holder covered a quarter of
  the frame had come out black.
