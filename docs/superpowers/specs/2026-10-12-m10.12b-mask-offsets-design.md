# M10.12b — A mask's scene sliders as offsets (Lightroom's model)

**Date:** 2026-10-12 · **Branch:** `m10.12b-mask-offsets` · **Status:** design approved

Phase 10's M10.12b: *"Masks' scene sliders (WB, exposure, contrast, tone regions) as offsets,
Lightroom's model — today they run after the global tone curve"* (raised at M7.3b, 2026-09-25).

## What was measured

`examples/probe_mask_offsets.rs`, DSCF6291 at 1200 px, under one ordinary global edit (Contrast 25,
Highlights −50, Shadows +30, Saturation 15, an S curve): each slider applied globally, and through a
mask covering the whole frame — what an offset and today's model give where a mask covers fully.

| slider in a mask | mean Δ | p99 Δ | pixels off by > 3 levels |
|---|---|---|---|
| Exposure +1 | **14.7 levels** | **85** | **77 %** |
| Temp 6500 K over a global 5000 K | 5.0 | 52 | 31 % |
| Contrast +30 | 3.6 | 49 | 29 % |
| Highlights −40 | 0.4 | 5 | 5 % |

**Why:** the tail runs the global white balance → exposure → contrast → tone regions → tone curve →
local contrast → saturation → HSL → grading, and only then each mask, which runs its OWN white balance,
exposure, contrast and tone regions on that finished look and blends by coverage. A mask's +1 EV
therefore lands after the S curve and Shadows had already shaped the darks at the lower exposure (the
right half of the probe's picture keeps them crushed). And a mask's Temp is an **absolute** Kelvin
(the global widget, starting at *As shot*) applied on top of the global correction — twice.

## Decisions (the author's)

- **Lightroom's model, true offsets** — not a move of the stages, not "leave it".
- **Range masks and auto-mask brushes select on the globally edited photo, before any mask** — for all
  of their sliders, one coverage per mask, so nothing chases its own effect. The cost (the global tail
  run a second time) is paid only where it is needed.

## Behaviour

- A mask's **Exposure, Contrast, Highlights, Shadows, Whites, Blacks** are **offsets**: per pixel, the
  value used is `global + Σ coverage_i × offset_i` over every active mask, applied at the global stage's
  own position. At full coverage a mask's +1 EV is exactly the global +1 EV there; at half coverage,
  +0.5 EV; overlapping masks add. Sums are not clamped (two +100 masks overlapping make +200, as two
  stacked masks always could).
- **A mask's white balance becomes a relative shift**: `temp` and `tint`, −100…+100, Lightroom's local
  Temp/Tint. `+temp` is warmer. `temp` moves the global white point by `MIRED_PER_STEP` = **1 mired per
  step** (±100 mired ≈ ±2,500 K around daylight — a first value, **the author tunes it on crops**); `tint`
  is in the global Tint's own units, one to one. Per pixel the gains are
  `G ⊙ exp(Σ coverage_i · ln R_i)`, where `G` is the global gains and `R_i = gains(global ⊕ shift_i) / G`
  — exact at full coverage of one mask, geometric between, and `exp(0) = 1` leaves `G` bit-exact where
  no mask covers.
- **An existing mask's absolute white balance is read as the shift it amounted to**:
  `temp = (mired(baseline) − mired(K)) / MIRED_PER_STEP`, `tint = tint` — exact under an as-shot global
  white balance, which is the only state in which the old model was not double-correcting. It is
  converted at render time (the baseline is the photo's), shown converted in the panel, and written in
  the new form the first time the mask's Temp or Tint is moved.
- **Unchanged:** every other mask slider — curve, Texture/Clarity, Saturation/Vibrance, HSL, grading,
  sharpening — still runs in `MaskStage` after the tail; masked noise reduction, dehaze and defringe
  stay prefix steps.
- **Selection:**
  - A mask whose every component depends only on **position** — linear, radial, a brush that does not
    gate, AI — has one coverage wherever it is computed; nothing new.
  - A mask with a **luminance or colour range component, or an auto-masked brush**, that carries a scene
    offset selects on the **globally edited photo, before any mask** — for its offsets AND its other
    sliders. Range masks with no scene offset select exactly as today.
- **No change at all** for a photo with no scene slider in any active mask: the tail is built exactly
  as today — byte-identical, at today's speed.

## Design

### Edit model (`edit-model/src/masks.rs`)

`LocalAdjustments` gains `temp: f32` and `tint: f32` (`#[serde(default, skip_serializing_if = …)]` —
M10.11's rule: a default writes nothing). `white_balance` stays, read-only in effect: a legacy absolute
value, converted at render time and cleared by the panel's first Temp/Tint edit.
`LocalAdjustments::has_scene_offsets()` — any of the six numbers or the shift (legacy or new) non-zero.
Pure conversion in `pipeline` (it needs `color`): `mask_wb_shift(a, baseline) -> (temp, tint)`.

### The stage (`pipeline/src/stages/scene_offsets.rs`)

`SceneOffsetsStage` — built by `build_pipeline_split_*` **in place of** the global white balance,
exposure, contrast and tone regions **when any active mask `has_scene_offsets()`**; otherwise the four
are built exactly as today. It holds the global values and, per contributing mask, its `Composite`
coverage (`stages::mask_coverage`, the one fold — invert and AI waiting included) and its offsets.
Per pixel: each mask's coverage; the summed values; then the same arithmetic the four stages use, in
their order, through functions the stages themselves now call (`exposure::factor`,
`contrast::apply`, `tone_regions::gain`, and the white balance gains above) — so where no mask covers,
the output is **bit-identical** to the four stages.

`MaskStage` stops running scene sliders: `adjustment_stages` for a mask builds no white balance,
exposure, contrast or tone regions stage (the global caller's own use is unchanged).

### GPU (`gpu-pipeline/src/scene_offsets.rs`)

`GpuSceneOffsetsStage`, a `REPLACEABLE` row and a field in `every_tail_stage()` (the warning in
`CLAUDE.md`). One shader: each mask's coverage chain generated by `mask_wgsl` (the expressions
`GpuMaskStage` already compiles, one function per mask), painted and AI components uploaded the way
`GpuMaskStage` uploads them; then the summed offsets and the four formulas. It declines what
`GpuMaskStage` declines (a gating brush without a map) — the CPU stage runs instead.

### The reference photo (part b2)

For a mask with a pixel-reading component that carries a scene offset, its coverage is computed from
the **globally edited photo**: the global tail — without any mask and without any offset — run on a copy
of the tail's input, by `SceneOffsetsStage` itself when such a mask exists. The coverage plane is
handed, through a shared slot built with the pipeline, to that mask's `MaskStage`, which uses it
instead of reading its own base. On the GPU those masks' coverage arrives as a prebuilt plane (the
AI-mask path); where that is not possible the twin declines.

## Delivery

- **b1** — the field, the conversion, `SceneOffsetsStage` (CPU and GPU), the panel's relative Temp/Tint,
  goldens rewritten. A pixel-reading mask with a scene offset reads its coverage, in b1 only, at the
  stage's own position — the state b2 replaces; never merged alone.
- **b2** — the reference photo for those masks, on both halves; the cost measured.

## Tests

- **The probe as a test:** under the probe's global edit, a mask covering everything with each offset
  renders like the global slider (within float tolerance; exposure, contrast, each tone region, temp,
  tint), and with **no** global edit too.
- **Where no mask covers**, the render equals the render without the mask — bit for bit.
- **Half coverage is half an offset** (exposure: `2^(0.5·ev)` at a pixel of coverage 0.5); two
  overlapping masks add.
- **No scene offset → the tail's stage list is today's**, and its renders byte-identical.
- **Legacy white balance:** a sidecar with a mask Kelvin renders, under an as-shot global, as it did.
- **GPU:** the twin renders like the CPU stage for every mask kind it accepts, and declines the rest.
- **b2:** a range mask with Exposure +1 selects the same pixels for its offset and for its Saturation —
  the chase made impossible; a range mask with no offset selects as today.
- **Goldens:** those with a masked scene slider are rewritten deliberately, the list of changed names
  checked to be exactly those; all others byte-identical.
- The panel: Temp/Tint ±100 with hover help; a legacy mask shows its converted shift; moving it writes
  `temp`/`tint` and clears the legacy value — one undo step.

## Out of scope

Offsets for the mask's other sliders — they stay where they run today, unmeasured here; the GPU computing the reference itself; Lightroom's local sliders this pipeline does
not have (local Dehaze in the tail, Moiré, Defringe as an offset).
