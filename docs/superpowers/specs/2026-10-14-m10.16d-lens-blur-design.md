# M10.16d — Lens Blur: a synthetic shallow depth of field on the depth map — design

**Date:** 2026-10-14 · **Branch:** `m10.16d-lens-blur` · **Status:** design approved in the brainstorm,
spec awaiting the author's review

Phase 10's M10.16d, added by the author on 2026-10-14 when M10.16a's brainstorm split *"Depth Range +
Lens Blur"* in two: a synthetic shallow depth of field on M10.16a's depth map, **next after M10.16a, ahead
of M10.16b/c**. It inherits M10.16a's open edge question: *clean cut-outs around people belong here*.

**Done when:** on a portrait, the subject stays crisp — hair and shoulders included — against a
convincingly blurred background (and foreground), with bright highlights blooming into discs of the
chosen aperture shape; focus can be put on the subject automatically or on any point; the blur follows
its sliders **live while dragging** (d2) and exports at full resolution.

## Decisions taken in the brainstorm (the author's)

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| What for? | **Portraits: subject separation** — a sharp person against a convincingly blurred background; a clean outline matters most. |
| Focus | **Both, as a choice:** *Subject* (automatic, the Subject network's outline keeps hair and shoulders crisp) and *Point* (a click; the depth map alone). |
| Bokeh | **Shapes too:** Circle, Blades, Ring, Cat-eye — and highlights blooming into discs (Boost). |
| Speed | **Live while dragging** — a GPU implementation, with a CPU reference for export and tests. |
| Approach | **A: a gather with a sampled aperture and three-layer occlusion.** Declined: B (depth slices + FFT: cat-eye impossible without tiling, FFT on the GPU means writing our own — realistically CPU-only, not live) and C (separable fast approximations: circles and hexagons only, rougher occlusion — fails the shapes requirement). |
| Scope | **One spec, two plans and two merges** (M10.12b's precedent): **d1** the CPU reference, the whole UI and export — correct, settling ~0.5 s after a Lens Blur drag; **d2** the GPU twin — live. **A spike first.** |
| Section 1 | Controls, defaults and the edit group — approved as presented (§1). |

## §1 What the user controls — the edit model

**A new Develop section, Lens Blur**, after Effects:
- **On** — a switch, off by default.
- **Amount** 0–100 — the strongest blur: at 100 the farthest background's discs reach **3 % of the
  photo's long side** (~190 px on an X-T4 frame); tuned once seen.
- **Focus: Subject | Point.** Subject focuses on the detected person; the Subject map's outline keeps them
  crisp. Point: a crosshair (the white-balance picker's shape) armed from the section; a click on the photo
  sets the focal plane to the depth there; the depth map alone draws the edges.
- **Focal range** 0–100 — how deep the sharp zone around the focus is; 0 = only the focal plane.
- **Bokeh** — Circle · Blades (6) · Ring (a mirror lens's donut) · Cat-eye (discs clipped toward the frame
  edges, like a fast lens's optical vignetting).
- **Boost** 0–100 — how strongly bright out-of-focus highlights bloom into discs.
- **Visualize** — M10.16a's depth view with the sharp zone tinted in the accent colour.

**Stored** — `Edits.lens_blur: LensBlur { on, amount, focus: LensFocus { Subject | Point { point: [f32; 2] } },
focal_range, bokeh: Bokeh { Circle | Blades | Ring | CatEye }, boost }`:
- the point **decode-normalised**, like an AI click — it stays on its object through crop and rotation;
- `#[serde(default, skip_serializing_if = "LensBlur::is_default")]` — **off is never written** (the
  `negative` / `grain` rule: a default block would change every rendered preview's key on disk);
- defaults: off; on → Amount 50, Subject, Focal range 10, Circle, Boost 25 (hand-written `Default`);
- its own edit group, **Lens blur**, **off by default** for Paste / Sync / presets — a focus point belongs
  to one photograph (Masks' and Geometry's reason); `merge_from`'s destructure and the three exhaustive
  fixtures gain it.

**What it needs:** the depth map always; the Subject map in Subject mode — both M10.16a's / M9.13's,
computed once, cached, and **requested automatically while Lens Blur is on** (`ai_coverage::queries`);
missing models open the existing download dialog.

## §2 The algorithm — the CPU reference

The CPU stage defines the picture; d2's GPU twin must match it.

**Inputs at the frame's render size:** the photo, linear, after geometry and the masked capture
corrections, before any tone; the **depth position** plane (0 near … 1 far); in Subject mode the
**Subject alpha** (the map through the AI mask's own Refine curve at 0 — soft, so hair strands keep their
partial cover).

**Focus depth `f`:** Subject — the median depth where the subject alpha > 0.5; Point — the 3 × 3 median
at the point (`depth_tool::depth_at`'s rule). Subject mode with no subject (no alpha > 0.5) falls back to
the depth at the frame centre and says so.

**Blur radius per pixel `c`:** 0 inside the sharp zone `|D − f| ≤ h` (h = focal range / 200, i.e. up to
half the depth scale), rising linearly to the full radius `R` (Amount × 3 % of the long side, at the
render scale) at `|D − f| = h + 0.5`; clamped. Subject mode: `c = 0` wherever the subject alpha is 1,
blended by it. **Background** = `D ≥ f − h` (the whole sharp zone and everything behind it), **foreground** =
`D < f − h` — every pixel in exactly one.

**Highlight boost:** before blurring, `B = I · (1 + 3 · boost/100 · s)`, `s` a smoothstep of luminance
from the frame's 99th-percentile luminance to three times it — linear light, where a highlight's energy is
physical. Mid-tones unchanged. ⚠ *Ruled while building d1:* a threshold below the percentile lifted a plain
mid-grey frame, every pixel of which is its own 99th percentile.

**The gather — dense, level by level ("scatter as gather"):**
⚠⚠ *Ruled while planning d1, replacing a sampled 128-point spiral:* a sparse pattern sees a point
highlight only where a sample lands on it, so a street light became a scatter of specks, not a disc.
- each **source** pixel `q` goes to the finest **level** (½, ¼, … of the frame) at which its disc spans at
  most `RCAP` = 8 working pixels; each level gathers **densely** — every working pixel within reach — from
  its own sources only, so the cost stays nearly constant whatever the photo's size;
- `q` contributes to `p` if **its own** aperture shape, scaled to **its own** radius `c(q)` and placed at
  **its own** position, covers `p` — `shape(p − q, c(q), position(q))`; that is what makes a highlight a
  disc and lets cat-eye vary across the frame. The disc edge is **anti-aliased** (2 × 2 sub-tests on the
  boundary) — a hard centre test made ragged, octagon-like discs;
- each contribution is weighted by `1 / area` — the analytic area, or for a disc under 3 working pixels its
  discrete footprint (an analytic area over-brightens a sub-pixel disc) — so **energy is conserved**: a
  small bright point becomes a dimmer, larger disc;
- the levels are upsampled bilinearly and summed (colour, weight, coverage) before the composite;
- **shapes** (normalised offset `o`, `|o| ≤ 1` the disc): Circle — `|o| ≤ 1`; Blades — inside the regular
  hexagon inscribed in the disc; Ring — the disc with an annulus weight (rim ≈ 1, centre ≈ 0.35);
  Cat-eye — the intersection of two unit discs offset by `±shift / 2`, `shift = 0.6 ×` the point's
  normalised offset from the frame centre (round at the centre, narrowing radially toward the edges;
  symmetric, so each disc stays centred on its own pixel).

**Occlusion — three layers, back to front:**
1. **Background:** gathers only from background pixels and is **normalised by the weight it found** —
   where the subject hides the background, it fills from what surrounds it, so the subject's colour never
   smears into the blur behind it (the halo).
2. **Sharp subject** (Subject mode): the photo itself, laid over with the Subject alpha — the crisp
   outline. Point mode has no separate layer: in-focus pixels are background with `c = 0`.
3. **Foreground:** its blurred colour **and** its blurred coverage (the gathered weight, clamped to 1)
   spread over everything — a near shoulder or a branch.

**Resolution:** the gathers run on the levels above (half resolution and coarser — blurred content has no
fine detail); the composite is at full resolution, and **where `c = 0` the output is the input, exactly** —
the blur never softens what is in focus.

**Place in the pipeline:** a **prefix step after geometry and the masked capture corrections** — cached, so
every tone and colour slider stays instant; a mask's adjustments run in the tail, after the blur. Its key
holds `LensBlur` and both maps' content ids (`AiRasters`, widened with a `plane(query)` for raw maps — the
depth's positions, the Subject's probability — beside `source(mask)` for a component's coverage). Export
runs the same step at full resolution.

## §3 The GPU twin (d2) and the canvas

**`GpuLensBlurStage`** (d2) — the same algorithm as compute shaders: per-pixel radius and layer; per level a
downsample of its sources and a dense background and foreground gather; the upsample-and-sum; the full-res
composite. The same levels, the same arithmetic — held to the CPU reference by a parity test, as every GPU twin is. It joins the prefix
seam by name (`"lens_blur"`, beside geometry and dehaze) with the resident hand-off; the geometry
checkpoint before it stays cached, so an Amount or Focus drag re-runs one step. **Target: under ~50 ms a
frame at the preview size**, measured with `RUSTROOM_DEBUG_GPU_TIME`. The maps upload once per content id.
No adapter → the CPU step. Until d2, a Lens Blur drag settles after ~0.5 s.

**Pick focus (Point):** a crosshair button in the section; armed, a click on the photo sets the point
(decode space, `ai_space::DecodeMap` — the Select click's path), **one undo step**, then disarms; Esc
cancels. A picker mode, like the white-balance one — no new Settings binding.

**Visualize:** a toggle in the section — the depth view (`depth_view`) with the sharp zone tinted in the
accent colour; view state, never `Edits`.

**Messages in the section:** "Estimating depth…" / "Finding the subject…" with a spinner while a map
computes (the blur appears when it lands); in Subject mode with no subject, *"No subject found — focusing
at the centre"*. Hovers one sentence, no full stop (`docs/DESIGN.md`).

## §4 The spike and the tests

**Task 4 of d1 is a spike** — a kept example, `examples/lens_blur_probe.rs`, on three of the author's
portraits (DSCF7121 the child, DSCF6760, DSCF7006 the jetty): CPU time at the preview size (1600 px) and at
full resolution; a sheet per photo — the original, Subject mode at Amount 50, Boost 0 vs 50, crops at the
subject's outline (the halo) and of highlight discs per shape. **Rules:** over ~1 s at the preview on the
CPU → tune the sample count or resolution before building on it; a visible halo at the outline → fix the
layer design first; **the look is the author's call** from the sheets.

**Measured 2026-10-15** (`lens_blur_probe`, release, Amount 50 Circle, the machine lightly loaded —
load ≈ 2.5), the CPU `render` alone:

| Photo | 1600 px | full size (6240 px) | radius at full | focus depth |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| DSCF7121 (the child) | 0.19 s | 3.39 s | 94 px | 0.21 |
| DSCF6760 | 0.19 s | 3.44 s | 94 px | 0.27 |
| DSCF7006 (the jetty) | 0.19 s | 3.52 s | 94 px | 0.21 |

Both rules pass: 0.19 s at the preview, **no halo** in any crop at the outline — the background fills
cleanly up to the hair; RCAP and the levels stay as built. ⚠ What the crops do show: the outline is only
as fine as IS-Net's Subject map — single strands against a plain wall take its hard edge (DSCF7121),
the same edge the Subject mask shows.

**Tests (d1)** — every key test sabotaged once; an inert sabotage recorded as inert:
- **edit-model:** a round trip; **off is never written** (`the_fingerprint_is_stable_across_calls_and_
  pinned` unchanged); the Lens blur group; `merge_from`; the three exhaustive fixtures.
- **the stage:** the radius is exactly 0 inside the sharp zone and monotone outside, Amount scales it;
  Subject focus = the median depth under the subject, Point = the 3 × 3 median, no subject → the centre;
  **energy conserved** — a bright point behind focus becomes a disc of the expected radius, its total light
  within ~2 %; **shapes** — a point light's disc is round (Circle), six-cornered (Blades), dim-centred and
  bright-rimmed (Ring), clipped toward the centre only off-centre (Cat-eye); **no halo** — next to a sharp
  red subject the blurred background beside its outline carries almost none of its red, while a foreground
  object does spread over the subject; **Boost** — a highlight's disc brightens, mid-grey does not move;
  **in-focus pixels come out identical** to the input; **off is byte-identical** (no step); the key carries
  both maps' ids; the prefix-step counting tests renumbered (CLAUDE.md's warning).
- **app:** with Lens Blur on the depth map — and in Subject mode the Subject map — is requested; Pick focus
  stores a decode-space point as one undo step; Visualize draws; the sliders are wired; the hover sweep.
- **goldens:** a synthetic scene (a depth ramp, a subject disc, point lights) — Subject + Circle, Subject +
  Cat-eye, Point.

**d2:** a GPU parity test per shape against the CPU reference; frame times on the author's photos with GPU
timestamps.

## Out of scope
- Refine brushes for the depth (Lightroom's Focus / Blur brushes) — the Subject outline is this design's
  answer to edges; brushes if use shows a need.
- Physical (metric) depth of field: the depth map is relative, so the blur grows with relative distance.
- Swirl, onion-ring and other lens-character bokeh beyond the four shapes.
- Lens Blur inside a mask.
