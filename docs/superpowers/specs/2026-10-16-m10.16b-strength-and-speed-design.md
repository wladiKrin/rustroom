# M10.16b — AI denoise: the strength and the speed (b3–b5)

An addendum to `2026-10-15-m10.16b-ai-denoise-design.md`. b1 (the `tract` path, the job, the cache, the
Amount) and b2 (DRUNet on the GPU) are built. The author tried them on 2026-10-16:

- *"It works, but it's painfully slow"* — then, on the GPU, *"still too slow to use it effectively"*.
- *"Even setting amount to 100 is quite weak … the difference is very subtle"* — about the same on every photo.

## What was measured (2026-10-16)

- **Speed.** A 26 Mpx frame: `tract` 397 s; the GPU 174 s (117 tiles of 512 px at ~1.46 s; the kernel at
  ~22 % of the M2's peak). The M2's adapter offers `SHADER_F16` and `SUBGROUP`.
- **Strength** (`denoise_probe strength`, four photos, ISO 160–3200). The noise rises toward the shadows on
  every photo (DSCF6990: σ 9.0 in the darkest eighth of brightness, 5.6 in the brightest unclipped one).
  Today's single σ matches the mid-tones. At Amount 100 it leaves 35–65 % of the noise in mid-tones and
  45–80 % in shadows. Of four variants on dark, mid-tone and busy crops — today, 2×, a per-brightness noise
  map, the map at 2× — **the map at 2× left the least everywhere** (0.03–0.9 σ).

## The author's calls (2026-10-16)

- **Strength: the noise map at 2×.**
- **Speed: your view first, plus a faster GPU** — over the Neural Engine, Metal-only matrix kernels and a
  lighter network.

## §1 Strength: the noise map at 2× (b3)

- **The noise curve.** `ai_denoise::noise_curve(img, enc) -> [f32; 8]`: σ for each eighth of encoded
  brightness, by today's estimator (2 × 2 binned, 16 px blocks, the flattest quarter, the median Haar
  diagonal) on that band's blocks only. A band with too few blocks, or reading under 0.5 (clipped highlights
  carry no noise), takes its neighbour's.
- **The map.** Per tile, each pixel's σ = the curve at its brightness × `CALIBRATION` (now **2.0**), clamped
  1…40. Its brightness is the encoded luma box-blurred over 15 px, so the noise itself does not set it.
  Linear between the bands' centres.
- **Both engines take a σ plane.** `TileNet::run` takes `&[f32]` (one σ a pixel) instead of a scalar;
  `GpuDrunet::run_map` exists; `tract`'s `Drunet` builds its fourth input plane from it.
- **The recipe moves** to `denoise:v2`: every result computed at 1× recomputes.
- **The Detail line** stays as it is; no new control.

## §2 Your view first (b4)

- **The order.** The job denoises the tiles nearest the view's centre first. The session publishes the view's
  centre in decode coordinates (its region mapped back through the geometry, the masks' `DecodeMap`) to the
  worker, which re-reads it between tiles — panning re-orders what is left.
- **Shown as it lands.** At most once a second, the worker publishes the partial result: the tiles finished
  so far, normalised by their own weights. `Denoised` carries the partial image and its coverage; the prefix
  step blends `in + (d − in) · amount` **only where covered**, and passes the rest through. Each publish has
  its own id, so the preview re-renders. The Detail line still counts the tiles.
- **Only a finished result is cached.** A partial one lives in the session's maps and dies with them.
  **Export never reads a partial result** — it loads a finished one or computes its own (b1's rule).
- **Fit view gains nothing** (the whole photo is in view); at 1:1 the visible region is ~15–20 tiles.

## §3 A faster GPU kernel (b5)

- **Half precision** where the adapter offers `SHADER_F16`: weights and activations stored as `f16`, sums
  kept in `f32` — half the memory traffic and half the workgroup memory. Without it, today's `f32` kernel.
- **Winograd F(2 × 2, 3 × 3)** for the 3 × 3 convolutions (58 of the 64): 16 products for each 2 × 2 block
  of outputs instead of 36, the input and output transforms around them. The 2 × 2 down and up convolutions
  stay as they are.
- **Each step measured** (`a_full_size_tile_time`, `editing_frames_while_a_tile_runs`) and kept only when
  ≥ 5 % faster with editing frames unharmed. **Estimate: 1.5–2.5× — ~70–110 s a photo.**
- **Parity:** `f16` and Winograd change the bits. The tolerance against `tract` is measured on the real model
  and written down; the author judges the look on the probe's sheets.

## Tests

- b3: the curve tracks synthetic noise rising toward the shadows; a flat curve gives a flat map; a fake
  network receives each tile's plane; the recipe moved; b1's tiling and blend tests still hold.
- b4: the order follows the view's centre, and a moved centre re-orders the rest; a partial result blends
  only where covered (an uncovered pixel is the input exactly); a partial result is never cached nor exported;
  a finished one replaces it.
- b5: per-kernel parity for the `f16` and Winograd paths against the reference; the small-network parity;
  the real-model parity within the measured tolerance; the `f32` path still taken without `SHADER_F16`.

## Out of scope

- The Neural Engine (Core ML), Metal-only matrix kernels, a lighter network — the author chose against them
  for now.
- A Strength slider — the map at 2× is the default and Amount blends below it.
