# M10.16b6 — AI denoise: FFDNet as the network, DRUNet in Settings, a quiet full-photo progress line

Branch `m10.16b6-ai-denoise-pan-and-network`, on b3–b5 (`m10.16b3-ai-denoise-strength-speed`), on b2, on b1.

## Intent

The author tried b3–b5 (2026-10-16): *"I think it works."* Three things remained:

1. *"It's a bit slower now to move around during the denoise. Like panning and zooming is noticeably slower."*
2. *"It would be nice to have a progress bar that says full denoising so that people understand that what
   they are looking at locally is already denoised, but it's still doing the full denoise in the background
   and it's a progress bar instead of percentages increasing."*
3. *"It's still quite slow, so I wonder if one can speed it up even more somehow."* — the wait that matters is
   **the view at 1:1 clearing** (~15 s on DRUNet), not the whole photo.

**Why:** AI denoise is judged at 1:1, Amount set, the result looked at, the photo left. Every second of that
wait is spent staring at noise; a result that arrives in a few seconds is a tool, one that takes 15 is a chore.

## Already done on this branch (measured, committed `a1a7d94b`)

Item 1, measured first (`bench_denoise_pan`, a 1:1 pan of DSCF6990 through the real `RenderEngine`): the cost
was a **stutter each time a partial result landed** — AI denoise is the prefix's first step, so a new result
re-ran it over the whole 26 Mpx frame, single-threaded on a 312 MB copy: **109–163 ms**. The job's GPU and
CPU sharing added +2–7 ms to a pan frame's median. The author chose the fix: the blend reads the shared
result in place, on every core — **45–52 ms** (one outlier 156 ms in 5). *Hold tiles while panning* was
offered and **not chosen**.

## The author's calls (2026-10-18)

- **The network: FFDNet at the noise map × 2** — from the spike's sheets (four photos, dark, mid-tone and busy
  crops, original · DRUNet · FFDNet × 2 · FFDNet × 1.5): *"I think the FFDNet at x2 works."*
- **DRUNet stays, as an alternative in Settings.**
- **The progress: right below Amount, inconspicuous** — and of three mockups, **C: "Full denoise" on its own
  line over a 3 px line across the whole section**, no percentage.

## The spike that chose the network (2026-10-18, throwaway, not committed)

- **FFDNet colour** (Zhang, Zuo, Zhang 2018; KAIR v1.0 release, MIT): `ffdnet_color.pth`, **3,411,551 bytes**,
  SHA-256 `286f04f7e3a20254c067ba95efb5d3339f4fc8a09a365a93cd469184d4cdb279`, from
  `https://github.com/cszn/KAIR/releases/download/v1.0/ffdnet_color.pth`. PyTorch's **legacy** format
  (protocol-2 pickles, then raw storages). 24 tensors: `model.{0,2,…,22}.{weight,bias}` — a 3 × 3
  convolution 13 → 96, ten 96 → 96, one 96 → 12, each with a bias, ReLU after all but the last.
- **The network:** the tile pixel-unshuffled 2 × 2 (channel `c · 4 + dy · 2 + dx`, KAIR's order) beside one
  plane of σ / 255 at the half resolution → 12 convolutions → pixel-shuffled back. The output **is** the clean
  image (not a residual). **~0.21 M multiplies a pixel against DRUNet's ~2.1 M.**
- **The port was checked:** synthetic noise of σ 25 on a smooth picture, 20.17 dB → **43.42 dB**.
- **The look (noise left, b3's unclamped estimator):** at the same map × 2 FFDNet removes more than DRUNet —
  0.04–0.52 against 0.09–0.92; FFDNet at × 1.5 matches DRUNet's flat-area residue. FFDNet holds the stained
  glass as DRUNet does; in flat dark areas at 200 % it leaves faint low-frequency mottling where DRUNet is
  smoother. The author chose × 2.
- **Speed:** the spike's naive CPU forward 1.7–2.1 s a 512² crop. On the GPU **estimated ~0.1 s a tile** from
  the multiply count — to be measured, not promised.

## Goals

1. **FFDNet colour is the AI denoise network by default** — on the GPU engine, with a CPU path of our own
   behind it — at today's noise map × 2 (`ai_denoise::CALIBRATION`).
2. **DRUNet stays selectable in Settings**, its results and its 130 MB model untouched for anyone who chose it.
3. **The full-photo progress line**, variant C, under Amount.
4. **The 1:1 view in a few seconds** — measured and written down.

## Non-goals

- Holding partial results while panning (offered, not chosen).
- NAFNet, the Neural Engine, Metal-only kernels, another network beside these two.
- A Strength slider, or a per-network calibration — both networks run the map × 2.
- Changing DRUNet's path in any way beyond routing to it from the setting.

## Anti-goals (a failure even if it works)

- **One network's cached result shown for the other** — switching must never pass off DRUNet's pixels as
  FFDNet's or back.
- **Code from a downloaded file executed**, in any form — the weights file is a pickle.
- **A loud progress indicator** — a percentage, the accent colour, its own panel or dialog.
- **A DRUNet user's results invalidated** by the switch arriving.

## Constraints

- Weights only through the existing model-download offer (`ai_mask::ModelId`, size and SHA-256 checked).
- **Licence:** KAIR's code and weights are MIT. FFDNet was trained on BSD400, the Waterloo Exploration
  Database and ImageNet validation images — whose own terms are research use. Whether that reaches the weights
  is as unsettled as DRUNet's DIV2K note; the registry's licence line says so.
- `docs/DESIGN.md` for everything seen; §7.16 (*"progress is never a bar of its own"*) gets this one
  exception, by the author's choice, written there.
- The repo's standing rules: placement decided first (`docs/PARALLEL-WORK.md` §5), no file over 1,000 lines,
  one cargo mode, never push, no `Co-Authored-By`.

## The design

### §1 The weights: a restricted reader of PyTorch's legacy format

- `ai_mask::ModelId::FfdnetColor` — file `ffdnet_color.pth`, the URL, size and SHA-256 above, the licence line.
- A reader of the legacy format beside `ai_mask::denoise::weights` (which reads ONNX initializers): the magic
  number, the protocol, the system info (little-endian required), the pickled state dict, the storage keys,
  then each storage's 8-byte count and its float32 bytes. The unpickler is a **closed subset**: the opcodes a
  protocol-2 state dict uses, and as globals **only** `collections.OrderedDict`, `torch._utils._rebuild_tensor_v2`
  and `torch.FloatStorage`; the persistent id `('storage', FloatStorage, key, location, count, None)`.
  Anything else is an error naming it. Tensors must be contiguous. Output: the same `Weights` (name → `Tensor`)
  `gpu_pipeline` already takes.
- Tests: a file **constructed** in the test (a two-tensor state dict written byte by byte) read back; a foreign
  global refused; a truncated storage refused; and an ignored test on the **captured** real file checking all
  24 names and shapes.

### §2 FFDNet on the GPU

- On the DRUNet engine's kernel (`gpu_pipeline::drunet`): 3 × 3 convolutions with `f16` storage and Winograd,
  the store's fusions, the bands that keep editing frames at ~15 ms. **New: a per-output-channel bias** in the
  store (DRUNet has none). The 12-layer chain is FFDNet's own program; ping-pong activations at half resolution.
- **The unshuffle, the σ plane (each 2 × 2 block's σ averaged, / 255) and the shuffle back run on the CPU** when
  a tile is packed and read — cheap beside the network, and no new kernel.
- `fits`, `release` and the error-on-non-finite rule as DRUNet's. Parity: against §3's CPU forward on a small
  random network (both precisions) and on the real file (ignored test, a measured bound written beside it).

### §3 FFDNet on the CPU

- Our own forward (the spike's, made production: CHW, rayon over output channels) behind a `TileNet` —
  the fallback when no GPU adapter exists, the GPU fails a tile, or Settings' *Use the GPU* is off. ~1.8 s a
  512² tile measured in the spike (DRUNet through `tract` was ~3–4 s).
- It is also §2's reference.

### §4 Tiles

- FFDNet sees ±24 px at full resolution (12 layers of 3 × 3 at half resolution). Its tiles' padding comes from
  that, confirmed by the existing seam test (`odd_sizes_tile_seamlessly`) run for FFDNet; tile sides stay
  multiples of 8 (FFDNet needs even). DRUNet's tiling is unchanged.

### §5 The setting

- **Settings → Performance, the AI denoise block:** a combo, *Network*: **FFDNet** (default) · **DRUNet**, one
  hover sentence. Stored as a new `Settings` field with its own default (FFDNet), following the file's rules for
  a field added after users have settings files (the `gpu_tail` trap noted there).
- The choice reaches **every** place that names the network: the job's factory (`denoised::network`), the
  download offer (the chosen network's model), export (`denoised::user_sources`), and the cache key.
- **The cache key carries the network.** DRUNet keeps today's recipe `denoise:v2`, so existing DRUNet results
  stay valid; FFDNet's is `denoise:v3-ffdnet` — the key pinned by a test like v2's.
- **A rendered preview's key carries the network** when Amount is on (`preview_key::PreviewKey` is the photo,
  the edits and the tier today — a switch would leave previews of the other network's pixels): a switch rebuilds
  those previews; photos without AI denoise keep theirs. *(Found by the builder read; my call.)*
- **A change with a photo open:** the worker is rebuilt (its network is made once per worker), a running job
  is dropped, and the photo shows the chosen network's cached result or runs it. *(My call.)*
- **Someone who has DRUNet but not FFDNet** (the author) gets the download offer once, as DRUNet's first use
  did: FFDNet is the default from the first launch.

### §6 The progress line (variant C)

- In Detail's AI denoise block (`develop/panel/ai_denoise.rs`), **while the open photo's whole-photo job runs**:
  under Amount, **"Full denoise"** in Caption `text2`, then a **3 px line across the section's content width**
  — the track `line`, the done part neutral (`text3`), never the accent; **no percentage**. One hover sentence on
  both: *"Your view is denoised first and the rest of the photo follows in the background"*.
- The fraction is the job's tiles done / total (`DenoiseWorker::progress`); the line appears once the job
  reports tiles — a result loaded from the cache never shows it. When the job ends the line goes;
  while it waits for the model or the full decode, or after a failure, today's text line stays.
- `denoise_tool::status` splits: a running job is a fraction for the line, every other state a text.
- `docs/DESIGN.md` §7.16 gets the exception: *AI denoise's whole-photo job draws a quiet line under its Amount
  (the author, 2026-10-18)*. The status row's job line is unchanged.

### §7 Measured and written down

A 512² tile on the GPU (FFDNet, `f16` + Winograd); the whole 26 Mpx frame; the 1:1 view's time; the parity
numbers; the pan probe during an FFDNet job (`bench_denoise_pan` gains the network as an argument).

## Testing
