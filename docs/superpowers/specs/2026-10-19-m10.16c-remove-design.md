# M10.16c — Remove: content-aware removal, Lightroom's Remove tool, locally

## Intent

P-26's last open gap: Rustroom has Heal and Clone spots (M5.3) — a circle copied from one source — but nothing
that removes an object by **filling it from its surroundings**. The author, 2026-10-19: *"just go ahead with
planning, try to do what lightroom offers already"* — he skipped the questions, so every call below is mine and
says so.

**What Lightroom Classic offers today** (its own help pages refuse automated reading; from the confirmed
facts and the tool's long-standing controls): a **Remove** tool with **Content-Aware Remove**, Heal and Clone;
the content-aware brush has **Size** and **Opacity**, a **Refresh** that tries another fill, **Detect objects**
(brush loosely, the tool finds the object), and **Use Generative AI** (Adobe Firefly, in the cloud — three
variations); **Distraction Removal** (June 2025) for **People** (detected, you pick which go) and
**Reflections**; and **Automatic Dust Removal** (October 2025).

## The how, decided

- **Local, never the cloud** *(my call — Rustroom's rule since M9.13: no photo leaves the computer)*. Lightroom's
  generative variations become **Refresh**: another fill from another context window, as many as wanted.
- **One milestone, four parts, built in order** *(my call — the b1–b6 precedent; each its own plan, each seen by
  the author before the next)*:
  - **c1 — the Remove brush:** content-aware fill by a local inpainting network, Refresh, Detect objects, Opacity.
  - **c2 — Dust:** sensor spots found automatically and healed — no network, the most value per cost.
  - **c3 — People:** the people in the photo found and offered; the ones picked are removed with c1's fill.
  - **c4 — Reflections:** a reflection-separation network — a spike first, built only if one runs here well
    enough; declined with numbers otherwise.
- **The network for c1 is chosen by a spike, the author picking from sheets** *(the FFDNet precedent;
  photographic judgements are his)*: **LaMa** (Apache-2.0, `Carve/LaMa-ONNX` `lama_fp32.onnx`, 208 MB, 512 × 512 —
  its Fourier layers rewritten for export, so whether `tract` loads it is the spike's first question) against
  **MI-GAN** (MIT, Picsart, `andraniksargsyan/migan` `migan_pipeline_v2.onnx`, 28 MB, plain convolutions), on his
  photos with objects masked, original · LaMa · MI-GAN, with times. Downloads only with his OK.

## c1 — the Remove brush

**What the user sees and does**

- Retouch's modes become **Remove · Heal · Clone · Red eye · Pet eye**, Remove first and the default *(my call —
  Lightroom's order)*.
- **Paint over the thing to remove** — Size as every brush (the mask brush's radius rule), one stroke one removal.
  On release the stroke shows as a hatched overlay while its fill computes in the background (a second or two at
  512), then the photo shows it removed.
- Per removal: **Opacity** 0–100 (default 100), **Refresh** (another fill), **Detect objects** (a checkbox for new
  strokes: the stroke grows to the object under it), Delete. Removals are listed and selectable like spots; a
  selected one shows its stroke; Visualize Spots shows them too.
- Undo/redo, copy/paste settings and presets carry removals like spots *(my call: presets don't — a removal is
  this photo's, as spots are excluded today; follow whatever spots do)*.

**How it works**

- **Stored in decode space** — `Edits.removals: Vec<Removal>`: the stroke (points normalised over the decode,
  radius as a fraction of its long edge — `BrushStroke`'s rule), `detect: bool`, `variant: u32` (Refresh's
  counter), `opacity`. `#[serde(default, skip_serializing_if = …)]`, its `EditGroups` group with the spots,
  `merge_from` and the three exhaustive fixtures *(CLAUDE.md's rule for a new field)*. Screen strokes map to the
  decode through the eyedropper's own `decode_point` path.
- **The fill is a patch:** a rectangle in normalised decode coordinates, its RGB and its soft mask at the crop's
  own decode resolution — resampled to the frame's size when pasted, so one fill serves every zoom rung and the
  export *(my call — the AI maps' rasters work this way)*. **Computed from the full-resolution decode**, waiting
  for it as AI denoise does *(my call: a fill from the half decode would be soft at 1:1)*. **Before a fill exists
  the step pastes nothing** — the photo shows the object under the hatched stroke until the fill lands.
- **The fill is computed once and cached** (the AI maps' pattern): from the **undeveloped decode** (through the
  negative's `positive`, *Warnings*), a crop around the stroke's bounding box with context — the box grown
  ~2.5× and at least 512 px of decode, the `variant` shifting the window — scaled to 512, the network run, the
  result scaled back. **Detect objects** first asks MobileSAM (`ai_mask::Sam`, already shipped for Select by
  click) with the stroke's box and points along it; the mask is SAM's object united with the stroke, grown a few
  pixels. Cached on disk under the photo's key, the stroke, `detect`, `variant` and the model; a job in the
  background like AI denoise's, never from a ghost session.
- **Applied as a prefix step right after AI denoise and before lens correction** *(my call: the fill is computed
  on the decode, so it is pasted in decode space; after the denoise step, or a denoised result computed from the
  un-removed decode would bring the object back)*: the fill pasted inside the stroke's soft edge at the removal's
  Opacity. Its `PrefixStepKey` carries every removal's content id (*Warnings*: a step reading derived data keys on
  its content). CPU — a paste; no GPU twin needed.
- **Export** uses the cached fills, computing a missing one there and then (the denoise export rule).

⚠ **Grain:** a network's fill is clean where the photo is noisy; with AI denoise on that matches, without it the
fill can look smoother than its surroundings. Measured on the spike's sheets; matching the noise is a follow-up
only if the author sees it.

**Measured and written down:** a fill's time on the CPU (512), the model's load, the cache's size per removal.

## c2 — Dust *(my call: before People — no network, a known-good classical method)*

- **Find dust:** in the decode, small, soft, darker-than-surroundings round blobs on smooth areas (sky, walls) —
  a difference of Gaussians on luminance, gated by local flatness and a size range in decode pixels, rated by
  contrast. Offered as a button in Retouch, **Find dust**, which adds a Heal spot per find (the existing spot
  machinery), each removable like any spot. **Visualize Spots** (M5.3) shows the result.
- A threshold slider for sensitivity *(my call)*. Measured on photos with real dust where the author has them.

## c3 — People *(designed 2026-10-21 from its spike; the author: "m with tiles, go ahead with the design")*

**The spike, measured** (`ai-mask/examples/people_probe.rs`, seven of the author's photos with people, `grep -n
'M10.16c3' docs/JOURNAL.md`): **YOLOX-m** (Apache-2.0, Megvii's own `0.1.1rc0` export, 101 MB) runs in `tract`. The
whole frame at 640 takes 0.43 s; 640² tiles over the frame at 1920 long, overlapping 160, take 3.7 s and found 44 more
people on the seven photos — the small, distant ones (12 more on the Luxembourg alley). **MobileSAM** outlines each
from its box: 1.8 s to embed the photo, 0.1 s per person. **LaMa** removes one in ~5.4 s. YOLOX-s found the same people
on the whole frame and one fewer in the tiles; the author picked **m with tiles**. Seen and said: shadows stay (the
outline stops at the feet), a dense far crowd is only partly removed, and a few outlines miss part of a person.
**Measured too:** MobileSAM outlining a person lying sideways — a portrait's decode orientation — agrees with the
upright outline at IoU 0.95–0.99 for people over 40 % of the frame's height, but only 0.62–0.94 for small ones.

**What the user sees and does** *(my calls, the author's to veto)*

- In Retouch's **Remove** mode, under the Removals: **Find people**. While it looks, "Looking for people" over the
  quiet progress line under the button (Remove dust's, `docs/DESIGN.md` §7.16); the button waits.
- Then every person found is **shaded on the photo with their outline**: the ones to be removed in the accent (set
  state), the others faintly neutral. **A click on a person toggles them.** Nothing else on the canvas reacts while
  people are shown — no stroke, no click-removal — so a click can only mean that.
- **Pre-ticked: everyone shorter than 35 % of the frame's height** — the subject of a portrait starts unticked. Right
  for the subject on all seven sheets; it also left two near bystanders on Notre-Dame unticked, a click each.
- The panel: "12 people · 9 to remove", an **Everyone** checkbox (all, none, or mixed), **Remove 9 people** (one undo
  step: each person its own removal, its row "Person 1", "Person 2"… in the Removals list) and **Cancel**. Either
  ends the shown people; so do Find people again, another mode or another photo.
- Nobody found: "No people found" stays under the button until the next press — Remove dust's rule.
- After Remove, each person is an ordinary removal — Opacity, Refresh, the eye, Hold out, Delete; while its fill is
  made, its area is shaded (c1b), which for a person is the outline the fill will replace.
- A press before the models are there opens the AI download dialog (YOLOX-m, and MobileSAM if missing) and waits;
  a press before the photo has loaded waits for it (Remove dust's rule). Never from a ghost session.

**How it works**

- **`ai_mask::people`** (no app): YOLOX-m loaded once; `find(&RgbImage) -> Vec<Found { bbox, score }>` — the whole
  frame at 640 and the tiles, merged; the spike's recipe written test-first: BGR 0–255 padded with 114, the output's
  stride-8/16/32 grid decoded, person = class 0, score ≥ 0.35, NMS at IoU 0.45 plus containment 0.7, a tile's find
  cut by an inner tile edge dropped, and a tile's find kept only where no whole-frame find covers it.
  `ModelId::YoloxM` joins the registry: `yolox_m.onnx`, 101,259,744 bytes, SHA-256 `21ff6cfdeb53b013bac2249599e55f00bff3cfdfdab37ed7a4620818c1d15b3f`
  (measured from the spike's download), Apache-2.0.
- **The search** (`people_tool`, Remove dust's shape: a thread, its progress, the finds once): the decode through
  `ai_worker::model_input` at 1920 long — the negative's positive, upright by the photo's quarter turns; YOLOX;
  MobileSAM embedded once over the same image and prompted with each box; each person's **area** (a rect and an
  alpha at 128², `remove_tool::Area`, c1b's shading) and **box** turned back to decode orientation. Pumped by
  Develop's body (`dust_flow`'s reason). The found people live on the session — view state, never `Edits`.
- **A person's removal: `Removal.person: Option<[f32; 4]>`** — the box, normalised over the decode,
  `#[serde(default, skip_serializing_if = "Option::is_none")]`, so no stroke removal's serialization changes. No
  points. Its window is built around the box (`hole_box`, `CONTEXT` 2.5); its hole is **only** MobileSAM's object from
  the box prompt over the window (no stroke to join), grown by `DETECT_GROW` and softened. Its key and `same_fill`
  carry the box; `hit_test` hits inside it; the overlay draws its box, lit when selected.
- **MobileSAM sees the window upright** (the measurement above): a fill's request carries the photo's quarter turns,
  the window and its prompt are turned before MobileSAM and its answer turned back. Detect objects' strokes take the
  same path, so **`RECIPE` → `remove:v3`** (the rule: any change to how a fill is made) — every cached fill is made
  once more, in the background.
- `missing_models`: a person's removal needs MobileSAM as Detect objects does; finding people needs YOLOX-m and
  MobileSAM. Export: as c1 — the cached fill, or computed there with the detector for a person too.

**Not in c3:** shadows — paint over one with the brush (a shadow finder is a follow-up only if the author asks);
dense crowds stay a weakness; *Select people* as a mask is not this.

**Testing**

- `ai_mask::people`: a constructed output tensor (one anchor per stride → the box it stands for); NMS and
  containment; the tiles' offsets cover the frame; a find cut by an inner tile edge dropped, one at the frame's edge
  kept; the merge keeps a tile's find only where uncovered; the real model on one of the author's photos — an
  ignored test, its path from an environment variable — finds a person scoring over 0.8.
- `people_tool`: a box and an area turned back to decode orientation for each of the four turns; the pre-tick by
  height; the removals made from the ticked people (one each, the box, no points); the search reports its progress
  and its finds.
- `remove_tool`: a person's window holds its box; its hole is the detector's answer only; the key and `same_fill`
  move with the box; the detector is handed the window and the prompt turned, and its answer is turned back — a
  stand-in that records what it saw, for each of the four turns; the Edits fingerprint unchanged.
- Panel and canvas: Find people → the line → "12 people · 9 to remove"; Remove 9 people adds nine removals in one undo
  step; Cancel adds none; "No people found"; a click on a person toggles them and paints nothing; Everyone ticks all
  and none.
- Export: a person's fill computed on export with stand-in networks.

## c4 — Reflections *(its own spike, possibly declined)*

- A reflection-separation network run once per photo like the depth map, an **Amount** slider blending the
  transmission layer back. Built only if a model loads in `tract`, runs in reasonable time, and the author likes
  its sheets; otherwise recorded as declined with numbers.

## Non-goals

- Cloud or generative services of any kind.
- Wires and power lines (Lightroom Classic has none either).
- Removing in other tools' spaces (masks' brushes stay masks).

## Anti-goals

- A removal that **reappears** — through AI denoise's result, a cache keyed without the stroke, or export.
- A fill computed on the **negative** of a film scan, or from a **ghost** session (*Warnings*).
- A fill that ignores **Refresh** (the same picture twice) or **Opacity**.
- Shipping a network whose licence forbids it.

## Testing

- c1: the stroke's decode mapping round-trips; the crop/context window and its `variant` shift; the paste inside
  the soft edge at Opacity (exact on a constructed fill); the prefix step's key moves with the stroke, `detect`,
  `variant`, opacity and the fill's content; a removal after AI denoise (the object does not come back); export
  with a cached and a missing fill; the cache key pinned; Detect objects' prompt built from the stroke; the real
  network on a constructed hole (an ignored test with the model: the fill closer to the hidden truth than the
  hole's mean).
- c2: dust found on a constructed image (soft dark discs on a gradient), none on texture; the spots it makes are
  Heal spots.
- c3/c4: each spike's numbers; tests in their own plans.
