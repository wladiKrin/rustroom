# M10.17a — HDR merge: a bracket into one floating-point raw, Lightroom's Photo Merge → HDR

## Intent

P-24's first half: Rustroom cannot combine a bracket. A scene brighter than one exposure holds — a window and
the room in front of it, a cathedral front against the sky — has to be shot as two or more exposures and merged
into one photo that keeps the highlights of the darkest frame and the clean shadows of the brightest. The author,
2026-10-26: *"just start"* — he skipped the questions, so every call below is mine and says so.

**What Lightroom Classic offers** (from its long-standing behaviour; not re-read from Adobe's pages): select
the bracket, **Photo → Photo Merge → HDR…** (⌃H; ⇧⌃H merges with the last settings, no dialog). A preview
dialog with **Auto Align**, **Auto Settings** (its Auto tone on the result), **Deghost Amount** None · Low ·
Medium · High, **Show Deghost Overlay**, **Create Stack**. **Merge** runs in the background; the result is a
**floating-point DNG** named after the first photo with `-HDR`, beside the originals, added to the catalog —
still a raw: white balance, camera profiles and lens corrections work on it, and its Exposure reaches further.

## The how, decided

- **Lightroom's shape, end to end** *(my call)*: the menu item, the dialog's five options, a background merge, a
  `-HDR.dng` beside the sources that opens as a raw of the same camera.
- **Raw only** *(my call — the one deliberate limit; yours to overturn)*. A JPEG's tone curve is the camera's
  secret, so a JPEG bracket can only be merged after guessing the curve back; a raw is linear by nature. A
  selection with a non-raw photo is refused with a word in the status row.
- **Merged in the camera's own linear space, after demosaic, before any colour conversion** *(my call)*: each
  frame decoded by libraw exactly as Develop decodes it, minus our camera matrix, with libraw's white-balance
  multipliers then divided back out. That is the space a DNG's raw data lives in, so the result **is** a raw of
  that camera: its white balance, camera profile (`.dcp`), lens profile and every edit work as on the
  originals. Probed 2026-10-26 on DSCF7618: after `dcraw_process`, `color.pre_mul` holds exactly the
  multipliers applied (`cam_mul` normalised to a minimum of 1 — 1.937, 1, 1.636), so the division is exact.
- **Written as a 16-bit floating-point DNG, read back by Rustroom itself** *(my call, forced by a probe)*. The
  libraw Rustroom bundles is built **without zlib**: on 2026-10-26 it opened a deflate-compressed float DNG for
  its capture facts, size and thumbnail — all correct — and then failed to unpack the pixels; an uncompressed
  one it read, but **clipped every value to ~1** (3.0 came back 1.07), which throws away exactly what an HDR
  is for. So `raw-decode` reads the pixels of a floating-point linear DNG itself and keeps libraw for the
  metadata and thumbnail, which it reads correctly. The file is a standard DNG 1.4 (half floats, Deflate,
  the floating-point predictor), so Lightroom, darktable and RawTherapee open it too.
- **A new crate, `photo-merge`, zero GUI** *(my call — CLAUDE.md's crate rule; M10.17b's panorama will join it
  and reuse its alignment)*. Driven headless by a new `rustroom-cli merge-hdr` and by the tests.
- **All CPU, rayon-parallel; no GPU twin** *(my call)*: it runs once per merge, in the background, and the
  decodes dominate it.

## What the user sees and does

- **Photo → Photo merge → HDR…** ⇧⌘H, and the same in a grid photo's right-click menu, on the selection (Library
  grid, Survey, or Develop's filmstrip). ⌘H is macOS's Hide, so ⇧⌘H rather than Lightroom's ⌃H *(my call)*.
  **Photo merge → HDR with last settings** ⌥⇧⌘H skips the dialog *(Lightroom's headless merge)*. A submenu,
  because M10.17b adds Panorama beside it.
- **Refused with one sentence in the status row** when the selection cannot be merged: fewer than two photos ·
  a photo that is not a raw · an Immich photo not downloaded to a folder (rename's rule) · photos from two
  cameras · photos of two sizes or orientations · a file missing.
- **The dialog** (Wide, §7.8 of `docs/DESIGN.md`), titled *HDR merge preview*:
  - A **preview** of the merged photo — rendered with the options as set — filling the body's left; until it
    exists, an empty well with the quiet progress line (§7.16's idiom: "Aligning", "Merging") *(my call — a
    third use of that exception, logged in DESIGN.md §13 in the same commit)*.
  - On the right, rows on the 130-pt label column: **Auto align** (on), **Auto settings** (on), **Deghost**
    None · Low · Medium · High as a segmented control (None), **Show deghost overlay** (off; red over what
    deghosting took from one frame alone), **Create stack** (off). Each with a one-sentence hover. Lightroom's
    defaults throughout.
  - A change to any option re-merges the preview in the background — Deghost and the overlay in well under a
    second (the aligned frames are kept), Auto align once more from its decodes.
  - Foot: **Cancel**, then **Merge**, the primary verb. The options are remembered in Settings when Merge is
    pressed (`start_import`'s rule).
- **Merge** closes the dialog at once. The merge runs in the background: the status row shows it with a
  **Cancel**, "Merging HDR · decoding 2 of 3", as an export does (§7.12). Several merges queue and run one at a
  time. Rustroom stays usable throughout.
- **When it lands:** `DSCF7618-HDR.dng` (the first photo in capture order, Lightroom's naming; `-HDR-2` when
  taken) beside it; catalogued; its edits seeded as any new photo's, plus Auto tone when Auto settings was on;
  stacked on top of its sources when Create stack was on (one undo step, the stack's own); selected in the grid
  when its folder is the one showing. "Merged DSCF7618-HDR.dng" in the status row. ⚠ The file itself is not an
  undo step — the same as an export.
- **Opening it** is opening a raw: Develop decodes it (Rustroom's reader), its Temp/Tint read the reference
  frame's as-shot light, the camera profile and lens corrections match the camera and lens, Exposure and
  Highlights find the highlights above 1.0 the brightest frame clipped.

## How it works

**The frames** (`photo_merge::frame`). Each photo is decoded with `raw_decode::decode_camera` (new): libraw's
demosaiced 16-bit output in camera space, white-balanced, its `pre_mul`, `cam_xyz` and `rgb_cam` beside it — the
same decode Develop runs, stopped before our camera matrix. **Kept as `u16`**, 156 MB a frame at 26 Mpx, half
an `f32` frame, because a merge may hold nine *(my call — no limit on the count; memory is stated, not capped)*.
A sample is **clipped** when any channel of its pixel reads ≥ `CLIP` (0.98 of 65535): libraw's highlight mode
clips a saturated channel to white. The divided-out value (`sample / pre_mul[c]`) is the normalised raw signal,
the same for every frame whatever its white balance.

**The reference.** The frame of the median exposure — EV from shutter, aperture and ISO; by mean brightness when
the files record none (a M9.10 DNG records none) — ties going to the active photo *(my call — Lightroom does not
say which it uses)*. It fixes the result's geometry, its exposure scale and its white balance; every other frame
is aligned and scaled to it.

**Auto align** (`photo_merge::align`). A dense, coarse-to-fine registration of each frame onto the reference
*(my call — over feature matching: one well-understood algorithm, sub-pixel by construction, and the frames of a
bracket overlap almost entirely)*:
- On **log luminance**, where an exposure difference is a constant: the model is a **homography** (handheld
  shift, rotation, a little perspective) plus one photometric offset — the frame's exposure relative to the
  reference — estimated together by Gauss–Newton (inverse compositional), robustly weighted (Huber) so a moving
  person does not pull it, and masked to pixels valid in both (not clipped, above the noise floor, inside).
- A Gaussian pyramid down to ~128 px; at the coarsest level an exhaustive search for the shift (±25 % of the
  frame); the first levels fit rotation and shift only, the finer the full homography — falling back to the
  similarity fit if the homography's residual is not better *(my call — a homography on a textureless region can
  wander)*.
- The dialog aligns its half-size decodes; the merge starts from those transforms (scaled ×2) and refines on
  the full decodes down to half resolution (`FINEST_LEVEL`, measured — full resolution if it pays).
- Each frame aligns **directly** to the reference; a frame sharing too little valid area with it (< 2 %)
  aligns through its neighbour in exposure instead.
- **Auto align off** — a tripod — keeps every transform the identity.

**Exposure** (`photo_merge::exposure`). Each frame's scale to the reference is **measured** — the alignment's
offset, then the median ratio over pixels mid-range in both — never taken from EXIF alone: nominal shutter speeds
are rounded (1/125 is 1/128) and libraw's `adjust_maximum` moves a clipped frame's white by a few per cent
(15361 of 16383 on DSCF7618). EXIF is the starting point and the fallback when too little overlaps.

**Deghost** (`photo_merge::deghost`), on a quarter-resolution luminance of every aligned, scaled frame:
- A **local reference** per pixel — the reference frame where it is valid, else the frame nearest it in exposure
  that is — so a region is always taken whole from one consistent frame.
- A frame **disagrees** where its scaled log luminance differs from the local reference's by more than the
  level's threshold (in EV), both above the noise floor; the disagreement is cleaned (a small blur, a threshold),
  grown by the level's radius and feathered into a 0–1 **ghost mask**, which removes that frame's weight there.
- **None** skips it. **Low · Medium · High** are a threshold and a radius each — starting at 0.6 / 0.4 / 0.25 EV
  and 0.3 / 0.6 / 1.0 % of the long edge, **named constants for the author to tune on crops**
  *(photographic judgements are his — the grain precedent)*. The overlay is the union of the masks.

**The merge** (`photo_merge::merge`), full resolution, rayon over rows. Per pixel, each frame's normalised raw
signal, warped into the reference's geometry (Catmull-Rom, `pipeline::resample`'s; a warp touching a clipped
sample counts as clipped), scaled to the reference's exposure, weighted by
- its **exposure** (a brighter frame has caught more light, so less noise — the noise-optimal weight for a
  photon-limited sensor),
- a **highlight roll-off** — full weight below 0.80 of white, smoothly to none at 0.96 *(constants)* — so no
  seam appears where one frame takes over from another,
- `1 − ghost`, and nothing outside the frame.
Where every frame is clipped, the darkest frame's value stands (a highlight no frame held). The result is in the
reference's normalised raw units: its mid-tones match the reference, its highlights run above 1.0.

**The file** (`export::dng_hdr`, sharing M9.10's IFD writer, split out to `export::tiff_ifd`):
- IFD0: a **1024-px preview** — the merged photo rendered with its seeded edits, 8-bit sRGB — carrying the DNG
  tags: `Make`/`Model`/`UniqueCameraModel` of the camera, `ColorMatrix1` = libraw's `cam_xyz` at **D65**
  (M9.10's finding: libraw reads any matrix as D65), `AsShotNeutral` = the reference's `1 / pre_mul`,
  `Orientation` 1 (libraw has already turned the frames), and an EXIF IFD with the reference's capture time,
  exposure, aperture, ISO, focal length, lens make and model — the probe showed libraw reads all of them back.
- SubIFD: the image, `LinearRaw`, 3 × **16-bit float**, 256 × 256 tiles, **Deflate with predictor 3**,
  compressed in parallel; `WhiteLevel` 1.0.
- Written to a dot-file beside the target and renamed into place, so a crash or Cancel never leaves half a DNG
  where the catalog will find it.

**The reader** (`raw_decode::float_dng`). `decode_raw` sniffs the TIFF: a `LinearRaw` image with
`SampleFormat` 3 is read here — tiles inflated (`flate2`), the predictor undone, half or single floats widened —
then the as-shot balance from `AsShotNeutral` (normalised to a minimum of 1, as libraw does), and the camera
matrix exactly as a raw gets it: `rgb_cam` from `ColorMatrix1` by dcraw's own `cam_xyz_coeff` (rows of
`cam_xyz · xyz_rgb` normalised, then inverted), then `camera_to_prophoto` — or the `.dcp`'s `profile_transform`
when one is chosen. `half_size` averages 2 × 2. Values are **never clamped**. Any other float DNG still goes to
libraw as today.

**The flow in the app.**
- `rustroom_app::hdr_merge` (lib, no egui): validating the selection into a plan (frames in exposure order, the
  reference, the output path), the options, and the **worker** — one thread, two kinds of job: a *preview* (half
  decodes kept for re-merges) and a *merge* (full decodes, the file); events for progress, a preview image and
  overlay, done, failed; Cancel checked between steps.
- `hdr_flow.rs` (bin): `RustroomApp`'s open/pump/finish — `scan_and_relink` the folder, seed the sidecar
  (`initial_edits(None)` + `auto_tone_tool::apply` when asked), stack (`stack_ids`, result first), select, the
  status line; the status row's running-job slot gains the merge with its Cancel.
- `modals/hdr_merge.rs` (bin): the dialog. `menu.rs`: `Command::MergeHdr` and `MergeHdrLast`; `commands.rs`
  dispatches; `library/cell_menu.rs` gains the item.
- `Settings.hdr: HdrOptions` — `#[serde(default)]`, Lightroom's defaults.

## Non-goals

- **JPEG / TIFF brackets** (above), **HDR panorama** (Lightroom's third merge — after M10.17b), **tone mapping as
  a separate step**: the merged raw is edited with Rustroom's own sliders, as in Lightroom.
- **Choosing the reference frame by hand**, **a deghost brush**, **merging a merged DNG** — none in Lightroom's
  dialog; a float DNG in the selection is refused.
- A GPU merge.

## Verification

- **Synthetic brackets** (`photo-merge` tests), with ground truth: a textured scene of known radiance spanning
  16 stops, rendered as 3 and 5 frames at ±2 EV with clipping and shot noise, warped by known homographies
  (a 13.4-px shift, 0.7°, a little perspective) — **alignment within 0.05 px at the corners, exposure within
  0.5 %, radiance within the noise wherever a frame held it, no step at the hand-overs**; a square moving in one
  frame — **gone at Medium, present at None** (the control); Auto align off with a shift present — the
  misalignment measurable (the control that the alignment is what fixed it).
- **The file:** write → `raw_decode` read back within half-float precision, values above 1 and near 0 included;
  libraw reads its capture, size and thumbnail; a deflate tile with the predictor, against a hand-checked tiny
  one.
- **A one-frame merge reproduces the raw** (`RAW_FIXTURE`-gated): one frame merged alone, written, read back
  through Develop's decode, equals the RAF's own decode within half-float precision — the test that proves the
  white balance, the matrix and the orientation all survive the round trip. And `cam_xyz_coeff(cam_xyz)`
  equals libraw's `rgb_cam` for that camera.
- **The app flow headless:** a bracket of M9.10 DNGs (libraw reads them; constructed, said so) → merge → the
  file catalogued, seeded, stacked, selected; Cancel leaves no file.
- **Real brackets** (`HDR_FIXTURE`-gated, and a probe with sheets): the author's three — **DSCF7618–7620**
  (Notre-Dame, a person posing and tourists moving: deghosting), **DSCF7621–7623**, **DSCF7753–7755** (a
  backlit window: an HDR scene) — all X-T4, handheld, ±⅓ EV. Measured: the time of a preview and of a merge, the
  file's size, the alignment's residual, and crops of None · Low · Medium · High with the overlay for the
  author to judge. ⚠ **None of the three spans more than 0.7 EV**, so they test alignment and ghosts but not a
  wide range; **a handheld ±2 EV bracket with something moving** (X-T4 AE BKG, three or five frames) would be
  the real acceptance — two minutes with the camera, if the author will shoot one.

**Estimated, to be measured:** a full-resolution X-T4 decode is 8–9 s (probed), so a 3-frame merge ~35 s and
its preview ~4 s; the file ~100 MB.

**Done when** the author has merged a bracket from the Library, seen the preview and the deghost overlay change
with the options, opened the `-HDR.dng` in Develop and recovered a highlight the reference frame had clipped, and
said "works".
