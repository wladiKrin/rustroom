# M10.17b — Panorama merge: a row of frames into one floating-point raw, Lightroom's Photo Merge → Panorama

## Intent

P-24's second half: Rustroom cannot stitch. A scene wider than the lens — a skyline, a valley, a room — is
shot as **several frames next to each other**, handheld, each overlapping the next, and has to become one photo
that is still a raw of the camera. The author, 2026-10-27: *"simply several pictures next to each other"*,
**3 to 6 frames, all raw**. What would make him wince — *"all of that"*: **a visible seam, a person cut in half or
doubled where two frames meet, a long wait**, and anything else that looks broken.

**What Lightroom Classic offers** (from its long-standing behaviour; not re-read from Adobe's pages): select the
frames, **Photo → Photo Merge → Panorama…** (⌃M; ⇧⌃M with the last settings, no dialog). A preview dialog with
**Projection** Spherical · Cylindrical · Perspective and **Auto Select Projection**, **Boundary Warp** (a slider),
**Fill Edges**, **Auto Crop**, **Create Stack**. **Merge** runs in the background; the result is a DNG named after
the first photo with `-Pano`, beside the originals, added to the catalog — still a raw. Its Auto Crop is a
**non-destructive crop** on the result, which the user can widen again.

**Built on M10.17a** (`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-26-m10.17a-hdr-merge-design.md` — read it first): the crate
`photo-merge`, the frames in the camera's normalised linear space, the dense aligner, the floating-point DNG
writer and Rustroom's own reader, the dialog's shape, the background worker, the status-row job.

## Decided by the author (2026-10-27)

- **The shape:** several frames in **one row**, handheld; select them, Photo merge → Panorama…, a preview dialog, a
  background merge into one raw file. *("sounds right")*
- **Edge treatment: Auto crop and Fill edges.** **No Boundary Warp.**
- **The quality bar:** no visible seam, nobody halved or doubled, not slow, nothing that looks broken.
- **3–6 frames, all raw.**
- **The result is capped at a 16,000-px long edge** — scaled down only when the stitched canvas is larger. Develop
  is not taught to open pictures of unbounded size in this milestone.
- **No real panoramas exist yet** (2026-10-27): everything is built and tested on synthetic panoramas first; the
  real-photo step waits for the author to shoot some.

## The how — my calls (each one the author's to overturn)

- **Lightroom's shape, end to end:** the menu item beside HDR…, the dialog, a background merge, a `-Pano.dng`
  beside the sources that opens as a raw of the same camera — exactly HDR's flow with a different body.
- **Raw only**, the HDR rule and the same reason: the result is a raw, merged in the camera's normalised linear
  space (`photo_merge::frame`, unchanged).
- **One row only.** The frames are arranged by their content, in any selection order, left-to-right or
  top-to-bottom; a selection that does not form one connected chain is refused naming the frame(s) that do not
  connect. Multi-row grids are a non-goal.
- **The lens is corrected BEFORE stitching, and baked in.** Distortion that is not removed makes two frames
  disagree away from their centres — a seam that cannot line up — and uncorrected vignetting is a brightness band
  at every join. So each frame is corrected by its LensFun profile (`lens_profile::Corrector`: distortion, TCA,
  vignetting) in the camera's linear space before it is projected. Consequence: **the result must never be
  lens-corrected again** — the DNG's EXIF **omits the lens make and model** (it keeps the focal length), so
  `auto_lens_correction` finds no profile for it, and its seeded edits carry `lens_correction` off. A frame
  whose lens has no profile is stitched uncorrected, with a quiet line in the dialog: "No lens profile for
  <lens> — distortion and vignetting are not corrected".
- **The camera model is a rotation about the lens** (a handheld panorama turns the camera, it does not move it):
  each frame is a 3-D rotation, all share one focal length. The focal length in pixels starts from EXIF ×
  LensFun's crop factor and is refined by the fit; a frame with no focal length (a manual lens) takes it from
  the fit alone.
- **Registration by features, then refined densely.** HDR's aligner cannot find a panorama's frames on its own: it
  searches shifts of ±25 % and assumes the frames almost cover each other, where a panorama's neighbours share
  20–40 %. So, new in `photo-merge`, built in-house (no OpenCV — the crate's own idiom, one dependency fewer):
  1. **Corners** on each frame's log luminance at a reduced size (~1500 px long edge): a Harris or FAST response,
     spread over the frame by keeping the strongest per cell.
  2. **Descriptors** that a brightness change cannot fool — normalised, oriented patches or a BRIEF-like binary
     test — and matching by nearest neighbour with a ratio test, both ways.
  3. **Every pair** (at most 15 for six frames) fitted by RANSAC to the rotation model; a pair is **connected**
     when enough inliers agree. The frames' order is the chain those connections form (a maximum spanning tree
     on inlier counts; for one row, a path).
  4. **A global fit** of every rotation and the focal length together over all connected pairs' inliers
     (Levenberg–Marquardt, a robust loss), so six frames do not drift.
  5. **Dense refinement** of each neighbouring pair's overlap with M10.17a's Gauss–Newton (`align`'s `init`
     path, restricted to the overlap), feeding sub-pixel matches back into the global fit — HDR's 0.05 px
     accuracy is the bar.
  The builder may swap step 1–2's detector for a better one if measured; the RANSAC + global fit + dense
  refinement shape is the decision.
- **Up is estimated from all the frames, never taken from the reference alone** ("wave correction", every serious
  stitcher's step): after the global fit, the result's up direction is the one perpendicular to the frames' common
  sweep — the direction their x-axes (for a horizontal row) share least — so a reference held 2° off level, or a
  sweep that wanders up and down, still gives a level horizon and vertical verticals. The **cylinder's axis
  follows the sweep**: vertical for a left-to-right row, horizontal for a top-to-bottom one; Auto select's angles
  are measured along and across that sweep.
- **Exposure matched by measurement** — never trusted from EXIF, the HDR rule: handheld frames on auto exposure
  differ, and even manual ones vary by a few per cent. One gain per frame, solved by least squares over every
  overlap's mid-tone pixels (log luminance), anchored on the **reference** frame. The raw signal is already
  white-balance-free (HDR's division by `pre_mul`), so one gain per frame is enough; a per-channel gain is the
  builder's to add if a measurement shows a colour step.
- **The reference frame** is the middle of the chain (the left of the two middles for an even count): it fixes the
  result's white balance (its as-shot light), its exposure scale and the projection's centre.
- **Seams cut where nothing differs, then blended by frequency:**
  - Between each pair of neighbours a **seam** is found through their overlap — a minimum-cost path from one
    side of the overlap to the other (dynamic programming on a reduced grid, then refined at full size near it).
    Its cost is high where the two frames **disagree** (their gain-matched log luminance differs — a moving
    person, a branch in the wind, a parallax error), where **either is clipped**, and a little on strong edges;
    low across flat sky and matching texture. So the seam goes **around** a person, who comes whole from one
    frame — this is deghosting for a panorama, and it needs no option.
  - The images are then joined by a **multi-band blend** (a Laplacian pyramid over the seam's masks, ~5 levels):
    a sky's gradient is blended over hundreds of pixels, a brick's detail over a few — so neither a brightness
    step nor a doubled edge shows.
  - A person who cannot be avoided — standing in the whole height of an overlap — is cut at the cheapest place
    the path finds; stated as a known limit, never hidden.
- **Projections** (the dialog's choice), all onto a canvas at the reference frame's pixel scale:
  - **Cylindrical** — the usual one for a wide row: verticals stay vertical, the horizon straight.
  - **Spherical** — for very wide or tall sweeps; bends straight lines more.
  - **Perspective** — a plane: straight lines stay straight, the edges stretch; only for a narrow sweep, and
    refused (with a sentence) past a total angle where it would explode (constant, ~120°).
  - **Auto select** (on by default) picks Perspective when the total horizontal angle is under ~70°, else
    Cylindrical, and Spherical when the sweep's vertical angle exceeds ~70° — **named constants for the author
    to tune** (the photographic-judgement rule).
- **Gaps in the canvas are never black.** A projected panorama has a ragged outline; its empty pixels are filled
  by smearing the nearest valid pixel outward (edge extension) before anything else sees them — because Develop's
  prefix (noise reduction, dehaze, lens blur) runs **before** the crop and would pull black into the picture at
  the crop's edge.
- **Auto crop is a non-destructive crop**, Lightroom's model: the DNG holds the whole canvas, and the result's
  seeded edits carry a `geometry.crop` to the **largest axis-aligned rectangle inside the valid outline**. The
  author can widen it in Develop and see the (smeared) edges, or keep it.
- **Fill edges bakes LaMa's fill into the DNG**: the empty outline outside the valid pixels, filled by M10.16c1's
  network from its surroundings. With Fill edges on, the crop is the whole canvas — so Auto crop is greyed while
  Fill edges is on (a hover says why). LaMa works on a 512² window; a panorama's empty margin is long and thin, so
  the fill runs **window by window along the outline**, each window scaled so the hole is at most about a third of
  it, the fill upsampled to full size and **given noise matched to its neighbours** so it does not look smoother
  than the photo around it. It reuses the Remove fill's encoding into and out of the network's range
  (`remove_tool`'s peak-channel encoding — a sky's blue clipped under luminance, M10.16c1). Needs the LaMa model:
  without it, Fill edges offers the same download Remove offers. `photo-merge` stays free of `ai-mask`: the
  inpainter is handed in as a trait object (`people_tool::Finders`' pattern).
- **All CPU, rayon-parallel; no GPU twin** — runs once per merge, in the background (HDR's call).

## What the user sees and does

- **Photo → Photo merge → Panorama…** ⇧⌘M, and **Panorama with last settings** ⌥⇧⌘M, beside HDR in the same
  submenu, and in a grid photo's right-click menu. ⌘M is the system's Minimise, so ⇧⌘M rather than Lightroom's ⌃M
  (HDR's reasoning). On Linux/Windows ⌘ is Ctrl, as everywhere.
- **Refused with one sentence in the status row**, HDR's rules: fewer than two photos · a photo that is not a raw ·
  an Immich photo not downloaded · photos from two cameras · photos of two sizes or orientations · a file missing ·
  a merged DNG (HDR or panorama) in the selection; and new: **photos at two focal lengths** (the shared focal length
  is the model). Frames that turn out not to connect are reported by the dialog (below), not the status row — it
  takes the matching to know.
- **The dialog** (Wide, `docs/DESIGN.md` §7.8), titled *Panorama merge preview*:
  - The **preview** fills the left: the stitched panorama, the Auto crop's rectangle drawn on it (outside dimmed)
    when Auto crop is on; zoom and pan as HDR's preview (M10.17a's last commits). Until it exists, the empty well
    with the quiet progress line: "Matching", "Aligning", "Stitching", "Filling edges".
  - On the right, on the 130-pt label column: **Projection** Spherical · Cylindrical · Perspective (segmented),
    **Auto select projection** (on — when on, the segmented control shows the pick and is disabled), **Auto crop**
    (on), **Fill edges** (off), **Create stack** (off). One-sentence hovers.
  - **Frames that do not connect** — the dialog says so in place of the preview: "DSCF0123 shares nothing with the
    others", Merge disabled. (A frame connecting to the chain only through a weak match is still merged.)
  - A missing lens profile is one quiet line under the options.
  - A change re-stitches the preview in the background: projection and crop from the kept rotations in about a
    second; Fill edges adds its fill.
  - Foot: **Cancel**, **Merge**. Options remembered in Settings on Merge (`Settings.panorama: PanoramaOptions`,
    `#[serde(default)]`).
- **Merge** closes the dialog; the merge runs in the background through the status row's job slot — "Merging
  panorama · decoding 2 of 5", with Cancel — **queued with HDR merges on the same worker** (one at a time).
- **When it lands:** `DSCF0120-Pano.dng` (first photo in capture order; `-Pano-2` when taken) beside it;
  catalogued; edits seeded as any new photo's **plus the Auto crop's rectangle and lens corrections off**; stacked
  on top of its sources when Create stack was on; selected; "Merged DSCF0120-Pano.dng". As HDR: the file is not an
  undo step.
- **Opening it** is opening a raw: white balance from the reference frame's as-shot light, the camera profile, the
  whole edit stack — and the crop, which the author can widen.

## How it works — where things go

- `photo-merge` (zero GUI), new modules beside HDR's — names the builder's, one responsibility each:
  `features` (corners, descriptors, matching), `rotation` (the camera model, RANSAC, the global fit),
  `projection` (the three maps and Auto select), `gain` (the exposure solve), `seam` (the cost and the path),
  `blend` (the multi-band blend), `crop` (the largest inscribed rectangle, edge extension), `pano` (the driver:
  decode → lens → match → fit → refine → project → seam → blend → fill → result; HDR's `hdr.rs` is the model),
  `fill` (the window walk along the outline and the `Inpainter` trait). Lens correction of a frame in camera space
  goes through `lens-profile` — a new dependency of `photo-merge`, not a copy.
- `export`: the panorama DNG is HDR's writer (`export::dng_hdr`) with the lens tags left out; generalise its name
  or its options, do not fork it.
- `rustroom-cli merge-pano` — headless, as `merge-hdr`.
- App: `rustroom_app::pano_merge` (lib, no egui: the plan, the options, the job kinds on the shared merge worker —
  generalise `hdr_merge`'s worker into one for both rather than starting a second thread), `pano_flow.rs` (bin:
  open/pump/finish, the seeding with the crop), `modals/pano_merge.rs` (the dialog), `menu.rs`:
  `Command::MergePanorama` / `MergePanoramaLast`, `commands.rs`, `library/cell_menu.rs`. ⚠ `session_flow.rs` is at
  987 lines — nothing goes there.
- **The size cap (the author's):** the canvas's long edge is at most **16,000 px** (`MAX_LONG_EDGE`) — the
  projection's scale is lowered so the whole canvas fits, before anything is rendered at full size, never a
  resample afterwards. Six portrait X-T4 frames (~4,200 × 6,200 each) at 30 % overlap would be ~18,000 × 6,500; the
  cap makes that ~16,000 × 5,800 ≈ **93 Mpx**, ~1.1 GB as `f32` RGB while blending, plus the `u16` frames
  (~156 MB each). The builder measures it; the blend runs in bands if it must. 16,000 is under the common 16,384
  texture limit, but a ~93-Mpx DNG is still the largest picture Develop has opened: opening one is part of the
  verification (a constructed one, then the author's), and a defect it exposes at **that** size is in scope; any
  size beyond the cap is not.

## Non-goals

- **Boundary Warp** (the author's call), **multi-row grids**, **HDR panorama** (each position a bracket —
  Lightroom's third merge, after this), **JPEG/TIFF frames**.
- **Placing frames by hand** when they do not match, **choosing the reference**, **a seam brush**, **a tripod
  shifting (not rotating) the camera** (parallax panoramas, flat-scan "mosaics").
- A GPU merge.

## Verification

- **Synthetic panoramas** (`photo-merge` tests, constructed and said so), with ground truth: a textured scene on a
  sphere (fine detail, a smooth sky gradient, straight lines), rendered as **3 and 6 frames** through known
  rotations and a known focal length, with 25–40 % overlaps, exposure differences of ±0.5 EV, vignetting, shot
  noise and one frame rolled 2°:
  - **the rotations within 0.05 px at the frame corners, the focal length within 0.2 %, the gains within 0.5 %**;
  - **no seam visible**: across every seam, the local mean and the gradient statistics match their surroundings
    within the noise (the control: the same test on a naïve averaged join fails it);
  - **a moving square** in one overlap: **whole once** in the result — never twice, never cut (the control: the
    straight-line seam cuts it);
  - the frames given in a **shuffled order** stitch identically; a frame of an unrelated scene is reported as
    unconnected;
  - **Perspective** keeps a straight line straight within a pixel; **Cylindrical** keeps a vertical vertical;
  - **Auto crop's rectangle** lies inside the valid outline and is maximal (checked against a brute-force search
    on small canvases); the smeared edge contains no black.
- **The file:** HDR's round-trip tests carry over; plus — the lens tags are absent and the seeded edits have the crop
  and lens corrections off.
- **Fill edges**: on a synthetic outline, every hole pixel filled, the valid pixels untouched bit for bit, the fill's
  noise level within a factor of the neighbours'; the inpainter is a stand-in in tests (LaMa itself is
  `LAMA_FIXTURE`-gated, M10.16c1's rule).
- **The app flow headless**: a constructed chain of M9.10 DNGs → merge → the file catalogued, seeded with the crop,
  stacked, selected; Cancel leaves no file; an HDR and a panorama queued run one after the other.
- **A capped-size DNG in Develop**: a constructed 16,000 × 5,800 float DNG opens, renders at fit and at 1:1, exports,
  and gets a rendered preview — before any real panorama exists.
- **Real panoramas** (`PANO_FIXTURE`-gated, and a probe with contact sheets for the author). **None exist yet**
  (2026-10-27): the plan builds and tests everything on synthetic panoramas and constructed DNG chains first, and
  stops at a marked hand-off asking the author to shoot **2–3** — at least one with **people moving** across an
  overlap and one with a **sky**; the tuning of the seam cost, Auto select's angles and the per-channel-gain
  question happens after. Measured:
  the preview's time, the merge's time, the file's size and pixel count, the seam paths drawn over the result, and
  crops at every seam for the author to judge.

**Estimated, to be measured:** a full-resolution X-T4 decode is 8–9 s (M10.17a's probe), so five frames ~45 s of
decoding plus the stitch — **about a minute for five frames**, in the background; the preview, from half-size
decodes at ~2,000 px, a few seconds after the decodes (~4 s each, in parallel).

**Speed — what is a bar and what is an estimate** *(my call, from the author's "a long wait")*. The waits he sits
through are bars: **an option change re-stitches the preview within ~1 s** (projection, crop — the rotations are
kept), and **the stitch after the decodes takes no longer than the decodes did**. The merge itself is in the
background and only estimated, but it is designed for speed from the start, not optimised later: the merge
**starts from the preview's rotations** (scaled, refined densely — never re-matched at full size), the seam is
found on a reduced grid, the blend runs in bands. Every figure above is measured and reported in the journal.

**Done when** the author has merged a panorama of his own from the Library, seen the preview change with the
projection and the edge options, found no seam and no halved person at 1:1, opened the `-Pano.dng` in Develop and
developed it like a raw (widening the crop once), and said "works".
