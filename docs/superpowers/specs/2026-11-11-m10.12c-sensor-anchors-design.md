# M10.12c — strokes, spots and eyes anchored in decode space

**Status:** the author said "just start" (2026-11-11), so the questions were skipped. Every decision below is
mine unless marked otherwise.

## Why

Brush strokes are stored in the space AFTER lens correction and perspective, just before geometry. Heal and
clone spots and red-eye circles are stored after lens correction and before perspective. So turning lens
correction on or off, or changing its distortion or scale, moves the picture under a stored mark while the
mark stays where it was. Strokes also move with a keystone change. The shift is about 0 at the centre and up
to about 3.5 % at the corners. On an X-T4 frame that is about 220 px, larger than a typical spot. The backlog
recorded it as blocked on "no forward lens map". M10.16c1 solved that for removals: `DecodeMap::post_lens_of`
inverts the corrector by fixed-point iteration, and eight steps land within a hundredth of a pixel.

**Success:** a spot, an eye or a stroke stays on the same content when lens correction (distortion, scale) or
perspective is changed after it was placed. A photo saved before this change renders exactly as it did
before.

## What changes

1. **Stored space.** `Spot.centre`, `Spot.source`, `Eye.centre` and `BrushStroke.points` are stored in
   **decode space** for new marks. That is the same space as `Removal.points`: normalised per axis over the
   undeveloped decode, in sensor orientation, before lens correction. Radii stay as they are, a fraction of
   the long edge. Over a normal lens the decode and post-lens frames differ in scale by well under 1 %, so I
   don't rescale radii.
2. **A per-item marker.** Each of the three types gains `decode: bool`, written as
   `#[serde(default, skip_serializing_if = "is_false")]`. A missing field is `false`, which means legacy: the
   item is in its old working space and renders exactly as today. New and moved marks write `true`. A saved
   file without anchored marks serializes byte for byte as before, so the pinned fingerprint holds. (My call:
   per item, not per `Edits`, because Copy/Paste settings, Sync and presets mix items from different
   photos.)
3. **One resolver, in `pipeline`, with zero GUI** (new module `pipeline::anchors`):
   - `Anchoring::new(lens: Option<&LensShot>, corrections, perspective, decode_w, decode_h)` holds the
     decode→post-lens map and the perspective homography.
     - The lens half is a **distortion-and-scale-only `Corrector` built at a small nominal size with the
       decode's aspect**. Lensfun normalises by the image's size, so a normalised point maps the same at
       any size. Building at about 1024 px keeps it to a few ms. The 290–370 ms full-size build must never
       happen per frame. It is memoised process-wide on the lens, the corrections and the aspect.
     - The perspective is `Homography::from_edits(p, aspect)`, which `SpotMap` already uses.
   - `resolve(&Edits, &Anchoring) -> Cow<Edits>` rewrites every `decode: true` item into its working space
     and clears the flag:
     - spots and eyes are taken to post-lens (the iterated inverse);
     - strokes are taken to post-lens, then through the homography forward.

     Legacy items and photos with nothing anchored pass through borrowed, with no copy.
   - `anchor_spot_point`, `anchor_stroke_point` and the like are the inverse, from working space back to
     decode. Gestures use them: a working-space point → `Corrector::source` (green) → decode, and for a
     stroke `Homography::source_of` first. This is the direction lensfun gives directly.
   - With no lens correction and no perspective, both directions are the identity.
4. **Every renderer resolves at its entry, once.** This follows the pattern of the render worker's
   "stripped here rather than at every call site, so no caller can forget":
   - the render engine's worker, where the scene-referred strip is;
   - the export render (`batch_export/render.rs`), which also serves rendered previews and print;
   - the CLI `render`.

   Downstream (prefix steps, `MaskStage`, the brush cache, GPU dabs) nothing changes: it still sees working
   space.
5. **The canvas.** The spot, eye and mask overlays and the hover and coverage tint draw the resolved
   positions. Every gesture that writes a position converts it back and sets `decode: true`: place, drag
   centre or source, paint a stroke, auto-source, Find dust's patches (which today go decode → post-lens and
   will now write decode directly), and the red-eye click.
6. **Migration on open.** When a photo opens in Develop and its lens is known, its legacy spots, eyes and
   strokes are converted to decode space with the edits as loaded. The picture is unchanged to within the
   iteration's hundredth of a pixel. There is no undo step, and the history keeps whatever it held, since
   legacy items still render. (My call: without this, every photo edited before today would keep the bug
   until each mark was touched.) A photo never opened again keeps rendering as legacy in export and is
   correct.

## Not changing

- Linear and radial gradients stay in frame space: they are compositional, not content-referred.
- Removals and AI maps are already in decode space.
- A paste onto another photo places decode-anchored marks by sensor position. That is as reasonable as
  today's post-lens position.

## Tests (each sabotaged once)

- **Spots follow the lens.** Use the synthetic lens fixture (`lens_grid`'s `b = -0.05`). Place a spot near a
  corner on a marked feature, render with correction on and off, and check that the patch lands on the
  feature both times, within 1 px. Today it is off by tens of px.
- **The same for a stroke**, through `MaskStage`'s coverage centroid, with lens and with a keystone. Also the
  same for an eye.
- **Round trip.** `resolve` after `anchor` returns the point within 0.01 px, over a grid that includes the
  corners.
- **Legacy unchanged.** A legacy item resolves to itself, bit for bit, and the goldens are unchanged.
- **Migration changes nothing.** A photo migrated on open renders within 1e-3 of the same photo
  un-migrated.
- **The fingerprint stays pinned.**
- **The cost.** Resolving 10 strokes of 2,000 points each costs well under a frame, measured and recorded.
